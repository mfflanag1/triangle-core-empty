## 1. Framework

### 1.1 Hypertrees and their codes

A **hypertree** is a set of agents, each with a sector in Z/3, and a set of teams, each made of
three agents, one from each sector, such that the incidence graph (agents and teams as nodes,
membership as edges) is a tree. Agents a, b are adjacent (a ~ b) when they share a team; in a
hypertree that team is unique. Every team e carries a cost ξ_e ∈ [0, ∞). Standing condition:

- **(R0)** every agent is in infinitely many teams, their costs are pairwise distinct, and
  only finitely many of them lie below any level.

An **incidence** is a pair (v, g) with v ∈ g. Given an agent b ≠ v, the incidence (v, g)
*points toward b* if g is the first team on the path from v to b, and *away from b* otherwise
(every incidence at b itself points away from b).

**Rank coding.** Let V = ∪_{n ≥ 0} (ℕ × {1,2})^n be the set of addresses, |v| the length of v.
In a rooted hypertree with (R0) the root o gets the address ∅. Its teams, in increasing order
of cost, are (∅,1), (∅,2), …; the two other agents of (∅,k) are (k,1) and (k,2), where (k,i)
is the one in sector s_o + i. Recursively, an agent v ≠ ∅ has a parent team e(v) (the first
team on the path to o), a parent agent p(v) and a sibling s(v) in it; its other teams, in
increasing order of cost, are (v,1), (v,2), …, and the other agents of (v,k) are (v,k,1),
(v,k,2), the i-th one in sector s_v + i. Write ξ_{v,k} for the cost of (v,k). The **code** is

    ω = (s_o, (ξ_{v,k})_{v ∈ V, k ≥ 1}) ∈ Ω := Z/3 × 𝒮^V,

𝒮 being the set of strictly increasing sequences in [0, ∞) that tend to infinity (a Borel
subset of [0,∞)^ℕ). Coding is a bijection between rooted hypertrees with (R0), up to
root-preserving isomorphism, and Ω. Ω carries its product Borel σ-field and is a standard
Borel space.

This σ-field equals the one generated by the truncated balls ω ↦ B_r(T_K, o), r, K ∈ ℕ, which
is the σ-field named in (H0) of LP_LOCALITY.md. (A truncated ball is a measurable function of
the code: it reads coordinates selected by comparisons with K. Conversely, truncation at K
removes the most expensive teams at each agent and so preserves ranks; hence {ξ_{v,k} ≤ t} is
the event that for all large integers K the ball B_{|v|+1}(T_K, o) contains the agent with
address v and that agent has at least k non-parent teams of cost at most t.)

**Decorations.** A decoration is a family of marks on teams or on incidences with values in a
Polish space (messages X_{v→g}, weights x_e, indicators m_e). In the code it is a family
indexed by addresses, so a decorated code lives in a countable product of Polish spaces.

### 1.2 Double roots and invariance

Let 𝔫 be a decorated code and b = (k,i) a neighbour of the root. Put ρ(𝔫, k, i) = (𝔫′, k′, i′),
where 𝔫′ is the code of the same decorated hypertree rooted at b and (k′, i′) is the address
of o in 𝔫′. Explicitly k′ = 1 + #{j : ξ_{b,j} < ξ_{∅,k}}, and every coordinate of 𝔫′ is a
coordinate of 𝔫 selected by finitely many such comparisons. So ρ is a measurable involution
of the space of doubly rooted codes. For measurable f ≥ 0 on that space write
f(𝔫; o, b) = f(𝔫, k, i) and f(𝔫; b, o) = f(ρ(𝔫, k, i)).

**Definition 1.1 (invariance).** A random decorated rooted hypertree 𝔑 is *invariant* if for
every measurable f ≥ 0

    E Σ_{b ~ o} f(𝔑; o, b) = E Σ_{b ~ o} f(𝔑; b, o)        (MT)

(the sum is over all (k,i); both sides may be +∞).

(MT) is hypothesis (H0) of LP_LOCALITY.md, and it is the only form of unimodularity that
tree.md, RDE.md and LP_LOCALITY.md use. "Unimodular", "invariant" and "satisfies the mass
transport principle" all mean (MT) in this file. Its equivalence with mass transport over
pairs of agents at arbitrary distance is not needed and not claimed.

**Lemma 1.2 (null sets transfer; complete).** Let 𝔑 be invariant and A a measurable set of
rooted decorated codes with P(𝔑 ∈ A) = 0. Then almost surely no re-rooting of 𝔑, at any agent,
lies in A.

Proof. Take f(𝔫; a, b) = 1{ξ_{e(a,b)} ≤ K}·1_A(𝔫 rooted at a). By (MT),
E #{b ~ o : ξ_{e(o,b)} ≤ K, 𝔑 rooted at b ∈ A} = E[1_A(𝔑)·2·#{e ∋ o : ξ_e ≤ K}] = 0. Let K → ∞:
a.s. no neighbour's re-rooting lies in A. Apply this to A_1 = {some neighbour's re-rooting lies
in A}, which is null, and induct on the distance. ∎

### 1.3 The Poisson hypertree

s_o is uniform on Z/3; (Π_v)_{v ∈ V} are iid Poisson processes on [0, ∞) with intensity
(ξ²/2) dξ, independent of s_o; ξ_{v,k} is the k-th point of Π_v. Since ∫_0^K ξ²/2 dξ = K³/6 is
finite and the total mass is infinite, the code lies in Ω a.s. This is (T, o, ξ). T_K is the
component of o after deleting the teams of cost above K.

**Lemma 1.3 (complete).** The Poisson hypertree is invariant.
Proof: the proof of Theorem A6 below with the message coordinates deleted. It uses only the
marking theorem and Mecke's formula, not the law μ. ∎

## A. The recursive tree process (gap (a); replaces RDE.md §1.3–1.5)

Throughout, (ξ_k) are the points of a Poisson process of intensity ξ²/2, and (X_k′, X_k″) are
iid with law μ, independent of it. Θ(μ) is the law of inf_k (ξ_k − X_k′ − X_k″) when this is a.s.
finite (Θ is RDE.md's map T; the letter T is kept for the tree). **Hypothesis: μ is a
probability law on ℝ with Θ(μ) = μ.** X, X′, X″, X_1, X_2, X_3 are iid μ, Y = X′ + X″,
S = X_1 + X_2 + X_3.

### A.1 What the fixed-point equation forces

**Lemma A1 (complete).** Put G(x) = E[(x + Y)_+³]/6. Then:

1. G(x) < ∞ for every x; G is continuous and nondecreasing, G(x) → 0 as x → −∞ and
   G(x) → ∞ as x → ∞; and P(X > x) = exp(−G(x)). In particular μ has no atoms.
2. Almost surely, for every x only finitely many k have ξ_k − X_k′ − X_k″ ≤ x, the infimum is
   a minimum, and it is attained at exactly one k.
3. Tails. Fix m with p := P(Y > −m) > 0. Then P(X > x) ≤ exp(−p(x − m)³/6) for x > m, and
   P(X ≤ −t) ≤ G(−t) = E[(Y − t)_+³]/6 for all t. Hence E|X|^n < ∞ for every n; in particular
   E|X| < ∞ and E S_+⁴ < ∞.

Proof. The points (ξ_k, Y_k), Y_k = X_k′ + X_k″, form a Poisson process on [0,∞) × ℝ with
intensity (ξ²/2)dξ ⊗ law(Y) (marking theorem). So N(x) := #{k : ξ_k − Y_k ≤ x} is Poisson with
mean ∫_0^∞ (ξ²/2)·P(Y ≥ ξ − x) dξ = E ∫_0^∞ (ξ²/2)·1{ξ ≤ x + Y} dξ = G(x) ∈ [0, ∞].

(1) Suppose G(x_0) = ∞ for some x_0. For x < x_0, (x_0 + Y)_+ ≤ (x + Y)_+ + (x_0 − x), so
(x_0 + Y)_+³ ≤ 4(x + Y)_+³ + 4(x_0 − x)³ and G(x) = ∞ too; G is nondecreasing, so G ≡ ∞. A
Poisson count with infinite mean is a.s. infinite, so N(x) = ∞ a.s. for every integer x and the
infimum is −∞ a.s. This contradicts Θ(μ) = μ being a law on ℝ. So G is finite, and
P(X > x) = P(N(x) = 0) = exp(−G(x)). Continuity and the limit at −∞ follow by dominated
convergence (dominating function (x_0 + Y)_+³), the limit at +∞ by monotone convergence, since
P(Y > −m) > 0 for some m.

(2) N(x) < ∞ a.s. for every integer x, and N is nondecreasing; so a.s. finitely many points lie
below every level and the infimum is attained. The values Z_k = ξ_k − Y_k form a Poisson process
on ℝ whose intensity measure has distribution function G (mapping theorem). G is continuous, so
this measure is diffuse and locally finite, and such a Poisson process is a.s. simple: no two
Z_k coincide. So the minimiser is unique.

(3) G(x) ≥ E[(x + Y)_+³; Y > −m]/6 ≥ p(x − m)³/6 for x > m. P(X ≤ −t) = 1 − exp(−G(−t)) ≤
G(−t). So X_+ has all moments, hence so has Y_+ ≤ X′_+ + X″_+. For the lower tail,
E(Y − t)_+³ ≤ E[Y_+³; Y > t] ≤ (E Y_+⁶)^(1/2)·P(Y > t)^(1/2) and P(Y > t) ≤ 2·P(X > t/2), which
decays faster than any exponential. So X_− has all moments. (The cruder bound
E X_− = ∫_0^∞ P(X ≤ −t) dt ≤ E Y_+⁴/24 of RDE.md §1.2 is also correct.) ∎

So the tail condition the construction needs, E Y_+³ < ∞, is a consequence of Θ(μ) = μ, not
an extra hypothesis.

### A.2 Construction of the inward messages

**Theorem A2 (complete).** There is a probability space carrying the Poisson hypertree
(s_o, (Π_v)_{v ∈ V}) and real random variables (X_v)_{v ∈ V} such that

- (a) for every n ≥ 0 the variables (X_v)_{|v| = n} are iid with law μ and independent of
  (s_o, (Π_w)_{|w| < n});
- (b) almost surely, for every v, X_v = min_k (ξ_{v,k} − X_{(v,k,1)} − X_{(v,k,2)}), and the
  minimum is attained at exactly one k.

The joint law of (s_o, (Π_v), (X_v)) is uniquely determined by μ.

For v ≠ ∅, X_v is the message X_{v→e(v)} from v into its parent team. X_∅ is an auxiliary
variable (the root's own minimum over all its teams).

Proof. *Depth-n laws.* For n ≥ 0 let E_n = Z/3 × 𝒮^{V_{<n}} × ℝ^{V_n}, where V_{<n} and V_n
are the addresses of length < n and = n; both are countable (infinite for n ≥ 1), so E_n is a
countable product of standard Borel spaces. Let P_n be the product measure on E_n under which
s_o is uniform, the Π_w (|w| < n) are Poisson processes of intensity ξ²/2 and the X_v (|v| = n)
have law μ. A countable product of probability measures needs no truncation of the cost level:
this is where the infinite degree is absorbed.

*Bonding maps.* Define Φ_n : E_{n+1} → E_n by keeping s_o and Π_w for |w| < n and setting, for
|v| = n, X_v := inf_k (ξ_{v,k} − X_{(v,k,1)} − X_{(v,k,2)}) (:= 0 where the infimum is −∞). Φ_n
is measurable.

*Consistency: Φ_n(P_{n+1}) = P_n.* Under P_{n+1} the blocks (Π_v, (X_{(v,k,i)})_{k ≥ 1, i = 1,2}),
|v| = n, are independent of one another and of (s_o, (Π_w)_{|w| < n}), because they are built
from disjoint sets of coordinates of a product measure. In each block Π_v is a Poisson process
of intensity ξ²/2 and the X_{(v,k,i)} are iid μ, independent of it. So X_v, a function of the
block of v, has law Θ(μ) = μ. Hence (X_v)_{|v| = n} are iid μ and independent of
(s_o, (Π_w)_{|w| < n}): this is P_n.

*Extension.* For N ≥ 0 let Q_N be the law on E_0 × … × E_N of
(Φ_0∘…∘Φ_{N−1}(η), …, Φ_{N−1}(η), η) with η ~ P_N. By consistency, the projection of Q_{N+1}
on E_0 × … × E_N is Q_N. The Kolmogorov extension theorem for a sequence of standard Borel
spaces gives a probability Q on Π_n E_n with these marginals. Under Q, a.s. η_n = Φ_n(η_{n+1})
for every n. So the coordinates s_o and Π_w of η_n do not depend on n > |w|, and with X_v := the
X_v-coordinate of η_{|v|} we get (a) (η_n ~ P_n) and the equation in (b) (the X_v-coordinate of
Φ_n(η_{n+1}) is the infimum over the level-(n+1) messages). By (a) at level |v| + 1 and Lemma
A1(2), the infimum is a.s. a minimum attained at a unique k; there are countably many v.

*Uniqueness.* By (b), for every n the family (s_o, (Π_w)_{|w| < n}, (X_v)_{|v| ≤ n}) is a.s. a
fixed measurable function of (s_o, (Π_w)_{|w| < n}, (X_v)_{|v| = n}), whose law is P_n by (a).
These families generate. ∎

Why an extension theorem is needed at all: the X_v are not claimed to be functions of the
costs (the process is not claimed to be endogenous; `redteam2/NOTES.md` gives numerical
evidence that it is not), so they cannot be built as limits on the Poisson probability space.

### A.3 Branches; independence on disjoint subtrees

A **branch** is an agent with all its descendants, their costs and their inward messages. Its
code is β = ((ξ_{v,k})_{v ∈ V, k ≥ 1}, (X_v)_{v ∈ V}) (no sector), a point of the standard Borel
space 𝔖 := 𝒮^V × ℝ^V; X(β) := X_∅(β) is its **root message**. For u ∈ V let 𝔛_u be the branch
at u in the process of Theorem A2, with addresses read relative to u. Let L be the law of 𝔛_∅.

**Proposition A3 (complete).**
1. For every n the branches (𝔛_u)_{|u| = n} are iid with law L, and independent of
   (s_o, (Π_w)_{|w| < n}).
2. For every antichain A ⊂ V (no element is a prefix of another) the branches (𝔛_u)_{u ∈ A}
   are independent, each with law L. In particular inward messages in disjoint subtrees are
   independent.
3. Recursive structure: 𝔛_∅ consists of Π_∅ and of the branches 𝔛_{(k,i)}, which are iid L
   and independent of Π_∅, together with X_∅ = min_k (ξ_{∅,k} − X(𝔛_{(k,1)}) − X(𝔛_{(k,2)})).

Proof. (1) Fix N ≥ n. For |u| = n the depth-N truncation of 𝔛_u, namely
((Π_w)_{w ⪰ u, |w| < N}, (X_w)_{w ⪰ u, |w| ≤ N}), is by Theorem A2(b) one fixed measurable
function of the input ((Π_w)_{w ⪰ u, |w| < N}, (X_w)_{w ⪰ u, |w| = N}). Under P_N these inputs,
for the different u of length n, are independent, independent of (s_o, (Π_w)_{|w| < n}), and
each has, after shifting addresses, the law of the corresponding input at the root under
P_{N−n}. So the truncations are iid, with the law of the depth-(N−n) truncation of 𝔛_∅, and
independent of (s_o, (Π_w)_{|w| < n}). The truncations increase with N and generate; a π-system
argument finishes. (2) It is enough to treat a finite antichain A; induct on the largest length
N of its elements. If ∅ ∈ A then A = {∅}. Otherwise group the elements of A by their first
letter (k,i). By (1) at n = 1 the depth-1 branches 𝔛_{(k,i)} are iid L. Inside one depth-1
branch the elements of A with that first letter form, in relative addresses, an antichain of
largest length at most N − 1; since that branch has law L, the induction hypothesis says their
branches are independent with law L. Different groups are functions of different depth-1
branches, hence independent. (3) is (1) at n = 1 together with Theorem A2(b). ∎

Independence at fixed addresses is not the whole story for messages in both directions: the
k-th cheapest team at an agent is not independent of the other teams there (those of lower
rank are cheaper). The correct two-directional statement is the Palm formula (P) of §A.5:
*seen from a typical team, its cost and the three branches hanging from it are independent,
and the branches are iid L.*

**Proposition A4 (finite cylinders: depth r, cost level K; complete).** Fix r ≥ 1, K > 0 and let
B = B_r(T_K, o). Call the agents of B at depth < r interior and those at depth r boundary. For
an interior agent v let W_v = min{ξ_{v,k} − X_{(v,k,1)} − X_{(v,k,2)} : ξ_{v,k} > K} (the *tail
message*). Then conditionally on B (shape and costs) the variables W_v (v interior) and X_u
(u boundary) are independent, X_u has law μ, and

    P(W_v > x | B) = exp(−G_K(x)),   G_K(x) = E[((x + Y)_+³ − K³)_+]/6.

Every inward message in B is computed from these by the finite recursion
X_v = min(W_v, min{ξ_g − X_w − X_{w′} : g = {v, w, w′} a child team of v in B}), and every
outward message in B by Definition A5 with W_v added to the minimum as one more candidate.

Proof. For r = 1: by A3(3) and the marking theorem, Σ_k δ_{(ξ_{∅,k}, 𝔛_{(k,1)}, 𝔛_{(k,2)})} is a
Poisson process on [0,∞) × 𝔖² with intensity (ξ²/2)dξ ⊗ L ⊗ L. Its restrictions to [0,K] × 𝔖²
and (K,∞) × 𝔖² are independent. B is a function of the ξ-coordinates of the first; given them,
its marks are iid L ⊗ L; W_∅ is a function of the second, and P(W_∅ > x) =
exp(−∫_K^∞ (ξ²/2)·P(Y ≥ ξ − x) dξ) = exp(−G_K(x)). For r > 1 apply the case r − 1 to each of the
(conditionally iid L) branches at the depth-1 agents of B. ∎

Consistency of these descriptions can be seen directly, and this is the only place where the
fixed-point equation enters: raising K to K′ splits W_v into the minimum of the tail message at
level K′ and the contributions of the teams with cost in (K, K′]; raising r to r + 1 replaces a
boundary message X_u by min(W_u, …), whose law has survival function
exp(−G_K(x))·exp(−(G(x) − G_K(x))) = exp(−G(x)), which is μ again.

### A.4 Messages in both directions

For a team g and v ∈ g write g∖v for the other two agents. The target is a family
𝔐 = (X_{v→g}) indexed by all incidences with

    X_{v→g} = min over teams g′ ∋ v, g′ ≠ g, of (ξ_{g′} − Σ_{w ∈ g′∖v} X_{w→g′}).        (R)

**Definition A5.** Inward: X_{v→e(v)} := X_v for v ≠ ∅. Outward, by induction on |v|:

    X_{∅→(∅,k)} := min_{j ≠ k} (ξ_{∅,j} − X_{(j,1)} − X_{(j,2)}),
    X_{v→(v,k)} := min{ ξ_{e(v)} − X_{p(v)→e(v)} − X_{s(v)},
                        min_{j ≠ k} (ξ_{v,j} − X_{(v,j,1)} − X_{(v,j,2)}) }     (v ≠ ∅).

Each is a minimum of a.s. finitely many values below any level (Lemma A1(2)), so it is finite
and attained, and each is a measurable function of 𝔛_∅.

**Lemma A5 (complete).** (i) 𝔐 satisfies (R) at every incidence. (ii) Let b be any agent. A
family satisfying (R) at every incidence, with all minima finite, is determined by its values
on the incidences that point toward b. (iii) Hence applying Definition A5 with b as root to the
toward-b part of 𝔐 returns 𝔐: the family 𝔐 is an object on the unrooted hypertree.

Proof. (i) For (v, e(v)) the teams g′ ≠ e(v) at v are its child teams, whose other agents send
inward messages: this is Theorem A2(b). For (v, (v,k)) the teams g′ ≠ (v,k) at v are the other
child teams and, if v ≠ ∅, the parent team e(v), into which p(v) sends X_{p(v)→e(v)} and s(v)
sends X_{s(v)}: this is Definition A5. (ii) Let (v, g) point away from b. In (R) the teams
g′ ≠ g at v are of two kinds. If g′ is not the first team on the path from v to b, both
incidences (w, g′), w ∈ g′∖v, point toward b. If g′ is that first team (v ≠ b), then g′∖v
consists of the next agent p on the path, with (p, g′) pointing away from b and p strictly
closer to b, and of a third agent s, with (s, g′) pointing toward b. So X_{v→g} is a function
of toward-b messages and of one away-from-b message at an agent closer to b. Induct on the
distance from b. (iii) follows from (i) and (ii). ∎

So each message is the stated function of the messages one step further out, in both
directions on every incidence, as required.

### A.5 Invariance

Let 𝔑 = (T, o, ξ, 𝔐) be the Poisson hypertree decorated with all messages; it is a measurable
function of (s_o, 𝔛_∅).

**Views from a team.** For a branch code β and k ≥ 1 let β^{(k,i)} be its sub-branch at (k,i),
and let β^{∖k} be the branch obtained by deleting the root's k-th child team with its two
sub-branches, re-indexing the remaining child teams by rank and recomputing the root message
as the minimum over the remaining teams. Both are measurable in β. For the team e = (∅,k) at
the root, with other agents b, c, the branches hanging from e at o, b, c are 𝔛_∅^{∖k}, 𝔛_b,
𝔛_c, and their root messages are X_{o→e}, X_{b→e}, X_{c→e} (Definition A5, first line).

**Palm formula (P) (complete).** For measurable F ≥ 0 on [0,∞) × 𝔖³,

    E Σ_k F(ξ_{∅,k}; 𝔛_∅^{∖k}, 𝔛_{(k,1)}, 𝔛_{(k,2)})
        = ∫_0^∞ (ξ²/2) dξ ∫ L(dβ_0) L(dβ_1) L(dβ_2) F(ξ; β_0, β_1, β_2),

and the same with s_o as an extra independent uniform argument on both sides.

Proof. Ξ := Σ_k δ_{(ξ_{∅,k}, 𝔛_{(k,1)}, 𝔛_{(k,2)})} is a Poisson process on [0,∞) × 𝔖² with
intensity Λ = (ξ²/2)dξ ⊗ L ⊗ L (Proposition A3(3), marking theorem). There is a measurable map
𝔟 with 𝔛_∅ = 𝔟(Ξ) (rank the points by ξ, attach the marks, take the minimum as root message),
and then 𝔛_∅^{∖k} = 𝔟(Ξ − δ_{x_k}), x_k the k-th point. Mecke's formula
E ∫ F̃(x, Ξ − δ_x) Ξ(dx) = ∫ E F̃(x, Ξ) Λ(dx), applied to F̃((ξ, β_1, β_2), φ) = F(ξ; 𝔟(φ), β_1, β_2),
gives the claim, because 𝔟(Ξ) = 𝔛_∅ has law L. s_o is independent of everything. ∎

**Theorem A6 (complete).** 𝔑 = (T, o, ξ, 𝔐) is invariant in the sense of Definition 1.1.

Proof. For an ordered triple (a_1, a_2, a_3) of distinct agents forming a team e define the
view

    𝔳(a_1, a_2, a_3) = (σ; ξ_e; β_1, β_2, β_3),

σ = (sector of a_1, of a_2, of a_3) ∈ Sym(Z/3), and β_i the branch hanging from e at a_i: the
agents separated from e by a_i, with their costs, coded by rank from a_i outward, and with the
messages on their incidences that point toward e. The branch codes do not depend on a root: in
the branch at a_i, "toward e" and "toward a_j" are the same direction for every j.

*Step 1: the doubly rooted network is a fixed function of the view.* There is one measurable
map Ψ with (𝔑 rooted at a_1, second root a_2) = Ψ(𝔳(a_1, a_2, a_3)) whenever a_1 is the root o
or a neighbour of o. Indeed, the hypertree, its sectors and costs are obtained by gluing the
three branches to a team of cost ξ_e; the messages pointing toward e are those carried by the
branches (the three messages into e being the root messages X(β_i)); the messages pointing
away from e are obtained from them by the recursion (R), outward from e, as in Lemma A5(ii).
By Lemma A5(iii) this reproduces 𝔐, whichever agent of e is the root.

*Step 2: the view measure is symmetric.* For o the root, e = (∅,k), b = (k,i) and c the third
agent, 𝔳(o, b, c) = ((s_o, s_o + i, s_o − i); ξ_{∅,k}; 𝔛_∅^{∖k}, 𝔛_{(k,i)}, 𝔛_{(k,3−i)}). Summing
(P) over i ∈ {1,2} and using that s_o is uniform and independent, for every measurable h ≥ 0

    E Σ_{b ~ o} h(𝔳(o, b, c)) = (1/3) Σ_{σ ∈ Sym(Z/3)} ∫_0^∞ (ξ²/2) dξ ∫ L^{⊗3}(dβ) h(σ; ξ; β_1, β_2, β_3)

(as s_o and i vary, (s_o, s_o + i, s_o − i) runs once through the six permutations). The right
side is unchanged when h is composed with the swap τ(σ; ξ; β_1, β_2, β_3) =
((σ_2, σ_1, σ_3); ξ; β_2, β_1, β_3), because L^{⊗3} is exchangeable and Sym(Z/3) is closed under
the transposition.

*Step 3.* 𝔳(b, o, c) = τ(𝔳(o, b, c)). By Step 1, f(𝔑; o, b) = (f∘Ψ)(𝔳(o, b, c)) and
f(𝔑; b, o) = (f∘Ψ)(τ 𝔳(o, b, c)). Apply Step 2 to h = f∘Ψ and h = f∘Ψ∘τ. ∎

The intensity (ξ²/2)dξ is σ-finite, not finite. That is harmless: (MT) is an identity between
elements of [0, ∞].

### A.6 The matching

**Definition.** A team e = {a, b, c} is in M iff ξ_e < X_{a→e} + X_{b→e} + X_{c→e}.

**Theorem A7 (complete).** Almost surely M is a perfect matching of T: every agent is in
exactly one team of M. (T, o, ξ, M) is invariant, its (T, o, ξ)-marginal is the Poisson
hypertree, and it therefore satisfies hypothesis (H0) of LP_LOCALITY.md.

Proof. For an agent v and a team g ∋ v put Z^v_g = ξ_g − Σ_{w ∈ g∖v} X_{w→g}. By (R),
X_{v→e} = min_{g ∋ v, g ≠ e} Z^v_g. The defining inequality of M reads Z^v_e < X_{v→e}; so for
each v ∈ e:

    e ∈ M  ⟺  Z^v_e < Z^v_g for every other team g at v.        (A7.1)

So at most one team of M contains v, and exactly one does iff the minimum of (Z^v_g)_{g ∋ v}
is attained at a single team. At the root, Z^o_{(∅,k)} = ξ_{∅,k} − X_{(k,1)} − X_{(k,2)}, and the
minimum is a.s. attained at a single k by Theorem A2(b). The event "the minimum at the root is
not attained at a single team" is thus a null set of codes of 𝔑, and by Theorem A6 and Lemma
1.2 it a.s. fails at every agent. (Directly: at v ≠ ∅ the minimum over child teams is X_v,
uniquely attained; X_v has the atomless law μ and by Proposition A3 is independent of
Z^v_{e(v)}, a function of costs and branches outside the subtree of v; so there is no tie.)
The definition of M is symmetric in a, b, c, so by (A7.1) the team chosen by a is also the team
chosen by b and by c: M is a set of pairwise disjoint teams covering every agent.

M is a measurable function of 𝔑 defined through incidences, with no reference to the root. So
every function of (T, ξ, M; o, b) is a function of (𝔑; o, b), and (MT) for (T, o, ξ, M) follows
from Theorem A6. ∎

M is a function of the tree and the messages. It is not claimed to be a function of (T, ξ)
alone; (H0) allows extra randomness.

### A.7 Cost and normalisation

**Theorem A8 (complete).** With c(M) := E Σ_{e ∋ o} ξ_e·1{e ∈ M} (the expected cost of the
root's team):

    E S_+³ = 6,     c(M) = E S_+⁴ / 8 = 9·E X.

Proof. By (P) with F depending on the branches only through their root messages, which are
iid μ, for measurable f ≥ 0 or f integrable,

    E Σ_{e ∋ o} f(ξ_e, X_{o→e}, X_{b→e}, X_{c→e}) = ∫_0^∞ (ξ²/2)·E f(ξ, X_1, X_2, X_3) dξ.

- f = 1{ξ < X_1 + X_2 + X_3}: the left side is E #{e ∋ o : e ∈ M} = 1 (Theorem A7); the right
  side is E ∫_0^{S_+} ξ²/2 dξ = E S_+³/6.
- f = ξ·1{ξ < S}: c(M) = E ∫_0^{S_+} ξ³/2 dξ = E S_+⁴/8, finite by Lemma A1(3).
- f = (X_2 + X_3)·1{ξ < S}. It is integrable: ∫(ξ²/2)·E[|X_2 + X_3|·1{ξ < S}] dξ =
  E[|X_2 + X_3|·S_+³]/6, and S_+³ ≤ 9·Σ_i (X_i)_+³, so this is at most a multiple of
  E|X|·E X_+³ + E|X|⁴ < ∞. So E[sum of the two partners' messages into the root's team]
  = E[(X_2 + X_3)·S_+³]/6. By exchangeability E[X_i·S_+³] does not depend on i, so
  E[(X_2 + X_3)·S_+³] = (2/3)·E[S·S_+³] = (2/3)·E S_+⁴, and the expectation is E S_+⁴/9.
- Let e* be the root's team in M, with partners b*, c*. By (A7.1) e* is the minimiser of Z^o,
  so X_∅ = Z^o_{e*} = ξ_{e*} − X_{b*→e*} − X_{c*→e*}. X_∅ has law μ (Theorem A2(a), n = 0) and
  all three terms are integrable. So E X = E S_+⁴/8 − E S_+⁴/9 = E S_+⁴/72. ∎

(Check of the first identity without the tree: G(X) is Exp(1) because P(X > x) = exp(−G(x)),
and E S_+³/6 = E G(X_3) with X_3 independent of X_1 + X_2 =(d) Y; so E S_+³/6 = 1.)

**Normalisation (complete).** In the market, a team's deficit is D(e) = 3 − v(e) and the
rescaled cost is ξ_e = q^(2/3)·D(e). Since P(D(e) ≤ t) = t³/6 for t ≤ 1, an agent has on average
q²·K³/(6q²) = K³/6 = ∫_0^K ξ²/2 dξ teams with ξ_e ≤ K (K ≤ q^(2/3)); this fixes the intensity
ξ²/2. A perfect assignment N has q teams, and every agent is in exactly one, so

    total deficit = Σ_{e ∈ N} D(e) = q^(−2/3)·Σ_{e ∈ N} ξ_e
                  = q^(1/3) · (1/q)·Σ_{e ∈ N} ξ_e = q^(1/3) · (1/(3q))·Σ_{agents v} ξ_{N(v)}.

So "the total deficit is c·q^(1/3)" means: c is the average cost per team, equivalently the
average over agents of the cost of the agent's own team. The tree quantity
c(m) = E Σ_{e ∋ o} ξ_e·m_e is exactly this (cost of the root's team): **cost per team = cost
per agent-triple = c; cost per agent = c/3** (each team's cost split equally among its three
agents). The same holds for a fractional x: Σ_e D(e)·x_e = q^(1/3)·(1/(3q))·Σ_v Σ_{e ∋ v} ξ_e x_e,
and c(x) = E Σ_{e ∋ o} ξ_e x_e. With this convention Theorem 3 of MAIN_THEOREM.md reads
limsup E(3q − LP*)/q^(1/3) ≤ c(m), and Theorem A8 gives c(M) = E S_+⁴/8 = 9·E X. There is no
stray factor of 3.

## B. The decomposition theorem (gap (b); amends tree.md §2, Theorem B)

### B.1 Deterministic statement

Let H be a hypertree (any countable degrees; (R0) and costs are not needed) and x a fractional
perfect matching: x_e ∈ [0,1], Σ_{e ∋ v} x_e = 1 at every agent v.

**Construction with root o.** Assign to every agent w a state t(w), a team containing w or the
cemetery †, as follows.

- t(o) = e with probability x_e (e ∋ o).
- Let w ≠ o have parent team f (the first team on the path to o) and parent agent p.
  If t(p) = f then t(w) = f. If t(p) ≠ f and x_f < 1, then t(w) = g with probability
  x_g/(1 − x_f), g ∋ w, g ≠ f (a probability vector, because Σ_{g ∋ w, g ≠ f} x_g = 1 − x_f).
- **Convention for x_f = 1.** If t(p) ≠ f and x_f = 1 the kernel x_g/(1 − x_f) is undefined;
  put t(w) = †. This event has probability 0 (Step 2 below), so the convention is never used
  and any other choice gives the same measure.
- **Independence.** Conditionally on (H, x) and on the states of all agents at depth ≤ n, the
  draws of the agents at depth n + 1 are independent. Equivalently t is a Markov chain indexed
  by the rooted tree with the transition rule above; its law on the product σ-field exists by
  the Ionescu-Tulcea theorem (one countable product of kernels per generation).

m := {t(w) : w an agent, t(w) ≠ †}, and μ^o_{H,x} is the law of m.

**Theorem B (amended; complete).** (i) μ^o_{H,x}-a.s. m is a perfect matching of H.
(ii) μ^o_{H,x}(t(w) = g) = x_g for every incidence (w, g); in particular μ^o_{H,x}(e ∈ m) = x_e.
(iii) μ^o_{H,x} does not depend on o; write μ_{H,x}. (iv) If φ : (H, x) → (H′, x′) is an
isomorphism then φ_* μ_{H,x} = μ_{H′,x′}.

Proof. *Step 1 (consistency).* If t(w) = g for one agent w of a team g then t = g at all three
agents of g: let p be the agent of g nearest to o; a child w of p through g has t(w) = g only
if t(p) = g, and then both children have state g. So m is a set of pairwise disjoint teams
covering every agent whose state is not †.

*Step 2 (marginals).* Induction on depth. At o it is the definition. For w with parent team f
and parent agent p: P(t(w) = f) = P(t(p) = f) = x_f. For g ≠ f: if x_f < 1,
P(t(w) = g) = P(t(p) ≠ f)·x_g/(1 − x_f) = x_g; if x_f = 1, then x_g = 0 and P(t(w) = g) = 0 by
the convention. In the second case P(t(p) ≠ f) = 1 − x_f = 0, so the convention is used with
probability 0, and P(t(w) = †) = 0. With Step 1 this gives (i) and (ii).

*Step 3 (root independence).* Let f = {o, o′, o″}. Under μ^o, (t(o), t(o′), t(o″)) equals
(f, f, f) with probability x_f and (e, g, h), all different from f, with probability
x_e·x_g·x_h/(1 − x_f)² (root: x_e; then o′ and o″ draw independently with the kernel). This is
symmetric in the roles of o, o′, o″, so it is also the law of the triple under μ^{o′}. Given
the triple, under either root the restrictions of t to the three branches hanging from f are
conditionally independent, and the restriction to the branch at a ∈ f is the chain started
from t(a) at a and run with the transition rule in which "parent" means "neighbour toward f".
That rule is the same for the roots o and o′, because inside the branch at a the direction
toward o, toward o′ and toward f coincide. So μ^o = μ^{o′}, and by connectedness μ^o does not
depend on o.

*Step 4 (equivariance).* φ carries the construction with root o to the construction with root
φ(o); use (iii). ∎

### B.2 On the Poisson hypertree

**Measurability of the kernel.** In rank coding the construction is a measurable function
m = 𝔪(ω, x, U) of the code ω, the weights x and iid uniform variables U = (U_v)_{v ∈ V}
independent of (ω, x): the agent at address v uses U_v and the cumulative sums of its kernel
along the rank order of its teams. For a finite set of addresses and a prescribed pattern on
it, (ω, x) ↦ μ_{(ω,x)}(pattern) = P(𝔪(ω, x, U) shows the pattern) is measurable by Fubini, and
such patterns generate the σ-field on matchings. So (ω, x) ↦ μ_{(ω,x)} is a
probability kernel, and by (iii), (iv) it commutes with re-rooting: the image of μ_{(ω,x)}
under the relabelling of addresses that ρ induces is μ at the re-rooted code.

**Theorem B′ (complete).** Let (T, o, ξ, x) be an invariant fractional perfect matching (x may
use extra randomness). Conditionally on (T, o, ξ, x) draw m from μ_{(T,ξ,x)}, independently of
everything else. Then (T, o, ξ, x, m) is invariant, m is a.s. a perfect matching,
E[m_e | T, o, ξ, x] = x_e for every team e at o, and c(m) = c(x) in [0, ∞].

Proof. *Mass transport.* For measurable f ≥ 0 on doubly rooted codes decorated with (x, m) put
F(𝔫; a, b) = ∫ f(𝔫, m′; a, b) μ_𝔫(dm′), where 𝔫 stands for (T, ξ, x); it is measurable because μ
is a kernel, and it is a function of the doubly rooted (T, ξ, x) alone because μ_𝔫 does not
depend on the root. Conditioning on (T, o, ξ, x) and using Tonelli,
E Σ_{b ~ o} f(·, m; o, b) = E Σ_{b ~ o} F(·; o, b) and E Σ_{b ~ o} f(·, m; b, o) =
E Σ_{b ~ o} F(·; b, o); the two right sides agree by (MT) for (T, o, ξ, x). *Matching and
marginals:* Theorem B (i), (ii). *Cost:* c(m) = E Σ_{e ∋ o} ξ_e·E[m_e | T, o, ξ, x] =
E Σ_{e ∋ o} ξ_e x_e, by Tonelli. ∎

Corollaries B1 and B2 of tree.md are unaffected.

## C. Weak duality on the tree (gap (c); amends tree.md §3, Lemma D)

**Lemma D (amended; complete).** Let (T, o, ξ, x, u) be **jointly invariant**: one random
decorated rooted hypertree, with weights x_e ∈ [0,1] on teams and prices u_v ∈ ℝ on agents,
satisfying (MT) for functions of all of (T, ξ, x, u). Assume Σ_{e ∋ o} x_e = 1 a.s.,
u_o + u_b + u_c ≤ ξ_e a.s. for every team e = {o, b, c} at the root, and **E|u_o| < ∞**. Then

    3·E u_o ≤ c(x) = E Σ_{e ∋ o} ξ_e x_e.

Proof. Transport f_±(a, b) = x_{e(a,b)}·(u_b)_±. By (MT),
E Σ_{b ~ o} x_e·(u_b)_± = E Σ_{b ~ o} x_e·(u_o)_± = E[(u_o)_±·2·Σ_{e ∋ o} x_e] = 2·E(u_o)_± < ∞.
So Σ_{e ∋ o} x_e·(u_b + u_c) is integrable with mean 2·E u_o, and

    3·E u_o = E Σ_{e ∋ o} x_e·(u_o + u_b + u_c) ≤ E Σ_{e ∋ o} x_e ξ_e. ∎

**Corollary D′ (prices that are a factor; complete).** Let h be a measurable function of
rooted codes of (T, ξ) and u_v = h(T rooted at v). If (T, o, ξ, x) is invariant then so is
(T, o, ξ, x, u), because every function of (T, ξ, x, u; o, b) is a function of (T, ξ, x; o, b).
So if u is dual feasible and E|h| < ∞, then 3·E u_o ≤ c(x) for every invariant fractional
perfect matching x, with or without extra randomness; that is, 3·E u_o ≤ a_tree.

Why the hypothesis is needed: the transported quantity x_e·u_b involves x and u together. If x
and u are each invariant with (T, ξ) but use separate extra randomness with an unspecified
coupling, (MT) for the pair is not given. Joint invariance, or u a factor of (T, ξ), is what
the proof uses. (A third sufficient condition, conditional independence given (T, ξ) with
root-independent conditional laws, is not written out here: **GAP, not needed**.)

**What was wrong.** The corollary takes "any sub-assignment x_q of the market" with cost and
deficiency bounds and says "apply x_q's rule to the δ′ market, rescaled". The δ′-market is not
uniform (P(δ′ > t) = (1 − t)^(1−η)), and an arbitrary measurable rule has no monotonicity in
its input law, so the assumed bounds, stated for the genuine market, do not transfer. If
instead x_q is kept as a function of δ = min(δ′, δ″), the uncovered set depends on δ″ and
step 3 of Theorem C (independence of S and δ″) fails.

**Corrected statement (complete; it is LP_LOCALITY.md Lemma 6 read as a lemma about an
arbitrary rule).** Let u, w be independent genuine markets, δ ∈ (0,1),
d_p = min(1 − (1 − u_p)^(1/(1−δ)), 1 − (1 − w_p)^(1/δ)); d is a genuine market. Let x be a
fractional packing of the q³ teams (x ≥ 0, every load at most 1) that is a measurable function
of (u, Z), where the extra randomness Z is such that the PAIR (u, Z) is independent of w.
(Corrected on 12 November 2026 after the Codex read: "Z independent of w" alone is not
enough; Z = (u + w) mod 1 is independent of w, yet (u, Z) determines w.) Let Σ = q − Σ_e x_e (the uncovered mass, the same in
each sector) and c_2 = sup_n E(3n − T_n)/n^(1/3) < ∞. Then

    E(3q − LP*(d)) ≤ E Σ_e D_u(e)·x_e/(1 − δ) + (c_2/δ)·(E Σ)^(1/3).

So if q^(−1/3)·E Σ_e D_u(e)·x_e ≤ a and E Σ ≤ ε·q, then
q^(−1/3)·E(3q − LP*) ≤ a/(1 − δ) + (c_2/δ)·ε^(1/3). If x is integral, the same bound holds for
E(3q − T): the uncovered agents form a set R with |R_1| = |R_2| = |R_3| = Σ, a function of (u, Z),
and x together with the w-optimal assignment of R is a perfect assignment of the d-market.
The proof is that of Lemma 6: the rule is built on the uniform market u, the patch uses w,
which is independent of (u, Z, rounding randomness) and hence of the uncovered set, and costs
are compared team by team through
D_d ≤ D_u/(1 − δ) and D_d ≤ D_w/δ. (No "+1/q" is needed: the rounding of Lemma 6 has
E N = Σ exactly.)
