## 0. Statement

Notation. L_q = 3q − LP* = min over fractional perfect matchings x of sum_e D(e)·x_e, where
D(e) is the deficit of team e. Rescaled cost xi_e = q^(2/3)·D(e). H_K^q is the hypergraph of
teams with xi_e <= K, each team marked by xi_e. T is the Poisson hypertree, T_K the
sub-hypergraph of its teams with xi <= K. B_r(G, v) is the rooted marked ball: all teams
containing an agent at distance <= r − 1 from v (agents are adjacent when they share a
team), up to rooted isomorphism. Phi(n) = E(3n − T_n) is the expected optimal integer
deficit of the n-agent market, Phi(0) = 0.

Hypotheses.

- **(H0) Invariance.** (T, o, xi, m) is a random rooted marked hypertree with the Poisson
  marginal, m a perfect matching (exactly one team of m at every agent), and the mass
  transport principle holds along teams: for every measurable f >= 0 of the network and two
  agents, E sum_{b ~ o} f(o, b) = E sum_{b ~ o} f(b, o). The measurable structure on rooted
  marked trees is the one generated by the maps (T, o, xi) -> B_r(T_K, o), r, K = 1, 2, ...
  m may use extra randomness. c(m) = E sum_{e ∋ o} xi_e·m_e.
- **(H1) Local convergence, annealed form.** For every K and r, with V a uniform random
  agent independent of the market, B_r(H_K^q, V) converges in distribution to B_r(T_K, o) as
  q -> infinity (space of finite rooted marked hypergraphs: same shape and close marks).
  This is implied by local weak convergence in probability. Only this weaker form is used.
- **(H2) Frieze–Pegden–Tkocz.** Phi(n) <= c_2·n^(1/3) for every n >= 1. (Fact 3 gives it for
  large n; small n are absorbed in c_2 since Phi(n) <= 3n. I have not opened the paper.)

**Theorem.** Under (H0), (H1), (H2), in the pair-additive market,

    limsup_q E L_q / q^(1/3) <= c(m).

Explicitly, for every delta in (0,1) and r >= 1,

    limsup_q E L_q / q^(1/3) <= c_r/(1 − delta) + (c_2/delta)·(eps'_r)^(1/3),

with c_r <= c(m) + 1/(3r) and eps'_r -> 0 defined in §2–§3 (tree quantities: the cost and
the mean root slack of a rule of range r + 1). c(m) = infinity is allowed (the claim is then
empty). The same holds in the Poissonised
model (§7) with factors (1 − delta)^(-1/3), delta^(-1/3) and c_2 = 4.87, where (H2) is
replaced by a two-line greedy computation.

Consequence: with Corollary B1 of `reports/tree.md`, a_LP <= a_tree (statement (L) there).

## 1. Lemma 1: the conditional matching is root-independent — complete

Let l_j be the j-th cheapest team at the root (costs at an agent are a.s. distinct and
locally finite). Put h_j = E[m_{l_j} | (T, o, xi)], a measurable function of the rooted
marked tree, and for an agent v and team e ∋ v put x^v_e = h_{rank_v(e)}(T, v, xi).

**Claim.** A.s., sum_{e ∋ o} x^o_e = 1, and x^b_e = x^o_e for every e ∋ o and b in e.
Hence x_e := x^o_e is an invariant fractional perfect matching, a function of (T, xi) alone,
with c(x) = c(m).

Proof. sum_j h_j = E[sum_{e ∋ o} m_e | T, o, xi] = 1. Null sets transfer to neighbours: if
N is a null set of rooted trees then E #{(e, b): e ∋ o, xi_e <= K, b in e, (T, b, xi) in N}
= E[1_N(T, o, xi)·#{...}] = 0 by (H0). Let psi(a, e, b) = sign(x^a_e − x^b_e), a bounded
measurable function of (T, xi) and the triple (it does not involve m), and transport
f(a, b) = psi(a, e, b)·(m_e − x^b_e) for the team e containing a != b (unique in a
hypertree). It is absolutely integrable: E sum_{e ∋ o} sum_{b in e\o} (m_e + x^b_e)
= 2 + 2 = 4, the second term by (H0). So (H0) applies to f:

- E sum_b f(b, o) = E sum_{e ∋ o} [sum_{b in e\o} psi(b, e, o)]·(m_e − x^o_e) = 0, because the
  bracket is a function of (T, o, xi) and rank_o(e), and x^o_e is the conditional
  expectation of m_e given (T, o, xi).
- E sum_b f(o, b) = E sum_{e ∋ o} sum_{b in e\o} psi(o, e, b)·(x^o_e − x^b_e) (condition on
  (T, o, xi); psi and x^b_e are functions of it) = E sum_{e ∋ o} sum_{b in e\o} |x^o_e − x^b_e|.

Equality of the two gives the claim. c(x) = c(m) by conditioning and monotone convergence.
QED

Sector labels play no role; they may be included among the marks or ignored.

## 2. Lemma 2: a finite-range packing on the tree — complete

Fix r and K = K_r = r. Let F_r = sigma(B_r(T_K, o)). These increase with r and generate
sigma(T, o, xi) by (H0). The truncation does not change ranks (it removes the most
expensive teams at each agent), and {xi_{l_j} <= K} is F_r-measurable. Define

    g_{r,j}(B) = E[m_{l_j} | B_r(T_K, o) = B] = E[h_j | F_r]   on {root of B has >= j teams},

a version with g >= 0 and sum_j g_{r,j}(B) <= 1 for every B. For an agent v and a team
e ∋ v with xi_e <= K put y^v_e = g_{r, rank_v(e)}(B_r(T_K, v)), and

    x^(r)_e = min over the three agents v of e of y^v_e   (0 if xi_e > K).

(a) Packing. sum_{e ∋ v} x^(r)_e <= sum_{e ∋ v} y^v_e <= 1.

(b) Range. x^(r)_e for e ∋ v, hence the slack s_v = 1 − sum_{e ∋ v} x^(r)_e in [0,1] and
the local cost sum_{e ∋ v} xi_e·x^(r)_e in [0, K], are functions of B_{r+1}(T_K, v).

(c) Cost. Since xi_{l_j}·1{xi_{l_j} <= K} is bounded and F_r-measurable,

    E sum_{e ∋ o} xi_e·x^(r)_e <= E sum_{e ∋ o, xi_e <= K} xi_e·y^o_e
        = E sum_{e ∋ o, xi_e <= K} xi_e·m_e <= c(m).

So yes: the min-packing costs at most c(m), for every r, with no error term.

(d) Slack. Write yhat^v_e = y^v_e·1{xi_e <= K}. By Lemma 1,

    s_o = sum_{e ∋ o} (x_e − x^(r)_e) = sum_{e ∋ o} max_{v in e} (x^v_e − yhat^v_e)
        <= sum_{e ∋ o} sum_{v in e} (x^v_e − yhat^v_e)_+ .

By (H0) with f(a, b) = (x^b_e − yhat^b_e)_+ the two neighbour terms have the same
expectation as the root term, so

    eps_r := E s_o <= 3·E sum_{e ∋ o} (x_e − yhat^o_e)_+ .

For each j, yhat^o_{l_j} = 1{xi_{l_j} <= K_r}·E[h_j | F_r] -> h_j = x_{l_j} a.s. (Lévy's
upward theorem and K_r -> infinity). The series is dominated termwise by x_{l_j}, which sums
to 1, so it tends to 0 a.s. and is bounded by 1. Hence **eps_r -> 0**. The slack caused by
the truncation at K is included in eps_r; there is no separate K-limit.

## 3. Lemma 3: an almost-everywhere continuous rule — complete

g_r is only a measurable function, defined up to null sets of the tree law, and (H1) is
weak convergence. Let O_r be the set of balls that are hypertrees with distinct costs at
every agent: open, of full measure under the law of B_r(T_K, o), and on it a ball is a
shape (countably many) plus a cost vector in an open subset of [0, K]^n, with a law
absolutely continuous on each shape. On each shape approximate g_r in L^1 by a continuous
function and compose with the Euclidean projection onto {y >= 0, sum y <= 1} (a
contraction). This gives G_r, continuous on O_r, zero off O_r, a sub-probability vector
everywhere, with E sum_{e ∋ o} |G_r − g_r| <= eta_r := 1/(9 r^2).

Run §2 with G_r in place of g_r. |min − min| <= sum of the three differences, and (H0)
moves the neighbour terms to the root, so the root slack changes by at most 3·eta_r in mean
and the root cost by at most 3·K_r·eta_r = 1/(3r). Let S_r and C_r be the slack and local
cost as functions of the (r+1)-ball under the rule G_r (defined for every finite marked
ball; agents whose r-ball is not in O_r get y = 0). Then

- S_r in [0,1] and C_r in [0, K_r] are continuous on O_{r+1}, an open set of full limit
  measure;
- E S_r(B_{r+1}(T_K, o)) =: eps'_r <= eps_r + 1/(3 r^2) -> 0;
- E C_r(B_{r+1}(T_K, o)) =: c_r <= c(m) + 1/(3r).

(If (H1) is available in total variation, this section is unnecessary: use g_r directly.)

## 4. Lemma 4: transfer to a finite market — complete given (H1)

Let u be a genuine market (pair deficits u iid U[0,1]), H = H_K^q(u), K = K_r. For v and
e ∋ v in H put y^v_e = G_{r, rank_v(e)}(B_r(H, v)) if B_r(H, v) is in O_r, else 0, and
x_e = min_{v in e} y^v_e (0 off H). Then:

(a) x is a fractional packing, a function of u, supported on teams with xi <= K.

(b) **Equal sector sums.** Every team has exactly one agent in each sector, so the total
load of each sector is sum_e x_e. With s_v = 1 − load_v, each sector has the same total
slack Sigma = q − sum_e x_e.

(c) s_v = S_r(B_{r+1}(H, v)) and sum_{e ∋ v} xi_e·x_e = C_r(B_{r+1}(H, v)). Summing over the
3q agents (each team is counted three times, each sector once):

    E Sigma = q·E S_r(B_{r+1}(H, V)),   E sum_e D_u(e)·x_e = q^(1/3)·E C_r(B_{r+1}(H, V)).

(d) By (H1) and the portmanteau theorem (bounded functions, continuous off a closed set of
limit measure zero): E S_r(B_{r+1}(H, V)) -> eps'_r and E C_r(B_{r+1}(H, V)) -> c_r.

Unbounded costs never appear: the rule ignores teams above K, and what that loses is slack,
already counted in eps_r. Agents with a non-tree ball (cycles, two near-tight teams sharing
a pair) contribute slack 1 each; their fraction tends to 0 by (H1) since the limit ball is
a.s. a tree.

## 5. Lemma 5: the split — complete

Let u_p, w_p be iid U[0,1] over the 3q^2 pairs p, all independent, and

    d'_p = 1 − (1 − u_p)^(1/(1−delta)),  d''_p = 1 − (1 − w_p)^(1/delta),  d_p = min(d'_p, d''_p).

Then P(d' > t) = (1−t)^(1−delta), P(d'' > t) = (1−t)^delta, P(d > t) = 1 − t: d is a genuine
market. By Bernoulli's inequality (1 − y)^a >= 1 − a·y for a >= 1,

    d_p <= d'_p <= u_p/(1 − delta),   d_p <= d''_p <= w_p/delta   pointwise,

hence D_d(e) <= D_u(e)/(1 − delta) and D_d(e) <= D_w(e)/delta for every team. The u-market
and the w-market are genuine, independent U[0,1] markets.

This replaces two items of the sketch. (i) No local convergence is needed for the
non-uniform d'-market: the main solution is built on the uniform market u, and the exact
cost factor is 1/(1 − delta) (in xi units the d'-market has intensity (1−delta)^3·xi^2/2,
so costs scale by 1/(1−delta); the factor (1−delta)^(-1/3) is the Poissonised one, §7).
(ii) No monotonicity of optimal costs is needed; the bound is applied team by team to a
fixed solution.

## 6. Lemma 6: patching — complete given (H2)

Let x = x(u) be any fractional packing with slacks s_v in [0,1] and common sector total
Sigma (Lemma 4b). Then

    E L_q(d) <= E sum_e D_u(e)·x_e / (1 − delta) + (c_2/delta)·(E Sigma)^(1/3).

Proof. Let U be uniform on [0,1), independent of (u, w). In sector i list the agents
v_1..v_q, let c_k = s_{v_1} + ... + s_{v_k}, and let R_i = {v_k : [c_{k−1}, c_k) meets
U + {0,1,2,...}}. Since s_v <= 1 each interval holds at most one point, so
P(v in R_i | u) = s_v. The size |R_i| = #{n >= 0 : U + n < Sigma} =: N depends only on
(Sigma, U), so it is **the same in all three sectors**, lies in {floor Sigma, ceil Sigma},
and E[N | u] = Sigma.

R = (R_1, R_2, R_3) is a function of (u, U), independent of w. So the w-deficits inside R
form a genuine N-agent market; let M_R be its optimal assignment (N disjoint teams covering
R). E[sum_{e in M_R} D_w(e) | u, U] = Phi(N) <= c_2·N^(1/3).

Put z_e = P(e in M_R | u, w) (average over U). Then z >= 0 and
sum_{e ∋ v} z_e = P(v in R | u, w) = s_v, so **x + z >= 0 has all loads exactly 1**: a
feasible point of the LP of the d-market. Therefore, pointwise,

    L_q(d) <= sum_e D_d(e)·x_e + sum_e D_d(e)·z_e
           <= sum_e D_u(e)·x_e/(1 − delta) + sum_e D_w(e)·z_e/delta.

Taking expectations, E sum_e D_w(e)·z_e = E Phi(N) <= c_2·E N^(1/3) <= c_2·(E N)^(1/3)
= c_2·(E Sigma)^(1/3) by Jensen. QED

Because the patch bound is concave in Sigma, only E Sigma is needed. This is why the
annealed form of (H1) suffices and no concentration is used. The feasible point depends on
(u, w), not on d alone; that is harmless, since L_q(d) is bounded pointwise.

## 7. Proof of the theorem, order of limits, Poissonised model

Fix delta and r. Lemma 6 with the packing of Lemma 4, then Lemma 4(c),(d):

    E L_q / q^(1/3) <= E C_r(B_{r+1}(H,V))/(1−delta) + (c_2/delta)·(E S_r(B_{r+1}(H,V)))^(1/3)
                    -> c_r/(1 − delta) + (c_2/delta)·(eps'_r)^(1/3)     (q -> infinity).

The left side's limsup does not depend on r or delta. Let r -> infinity (Lemmas 2, 3):
limsup <= c(m)/(1 − delta). Let delta -> 0. The bound is for the expectation. QED

**Poissonised model.** Assumed definition (I could not read the other folders): the q^3
team deficits are iid with P(D <= t) = min(1, t^3/6). Changes:

- Split at team level: D' = F^(-1)(1 − (1−u_e)^(1/(1−delta))) <= (1 − delta)^(-1/3)·D_u(e) and
  D'' <= delta^(-1/3)·D_w(e), since F^(-1)(p) = (6p)^(1/3); min(D', D'') has law F.
- (H2) is replaced by the greedy bound: sector-1 agents choose in turn the cheapest team
  among unused partners; the k^2 candidate costs are fresh, E min <= (6/k^2)^(1/3)·Gamma(4/3),
  and sum_{k<=n} k^(-2/3) <= 3 n^(1/3). So Phi_P(n) <= 3·6^(1/3)·Gamma(4/3)·n^(1/3) < 4.87·n^(1/3)
  for all n. **Complete.**
- (H1) for the Poissonised hypergraph (standard; stated as a hypothesis here).

Result: limsup E L_q/q^(1/3) <= c_r·(1−delta)^(-1/3) + 4.87·delta^(-1/3)·(eps'_r)^(1/3) -> c(m).
If F is only cubic near 0, the factors hold on the near-tight range and the greedy bound
gains an additive constant C(delta, F); the conclusion is unchanged (not written out: gap,
minor).
