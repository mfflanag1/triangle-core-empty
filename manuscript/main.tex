\title{The core of a random three-sided assignment market is empty}
\author{Draft --- author to be determined}
\date{12 November 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Take three sectors of $q$ agents each. Every pair of agents from different sectors has an
independent value, uniform on $[0,1]$, and a team of one agent from each sector is worth the sum
of its three pair values. We show that the core of the resulting three-sided assignment game is
empty with probability tending to $1$ as $q\to\infty$. The core is nonempty exactly when the gap
$\Dq$ between the optimum of the fractional relaxation of the assignment problem and the value of
the best assignment is zero, and we prove that $\E\Dq\ge(0.050-o(1))\,q^{1/3}$ and that
$\Dq/\E\Dq\to1$ in probability. The proof compares two constants on the scale $q^{1/3}$. With
high probability every assignment falls short of the trivial bound $3q$ by at least
$\betaint q^{1/3}$, $\betaint=2.302$, by a first moment conditioned on the local stars of all
agents. The fractional
optimum falls short in expectation by at most $(\alphalp+o(1))q^{1/3}$, $\alphalp=2.2519$,
because a random
perfect matching on the limiting Poisson hypertree, built from a solution of a recursive
distributional equation, can be carried to the finite market as a fractional solution. The
solution exists by a monotonicity property of the recursion in the dispersive order. Both
constants rest on computations in interval arithmetic. All statements are asymptotic and say
nothing at any size that can be simulated.
\end{abstract}
