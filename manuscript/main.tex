\title[Asymptotic core emptiness]{Asymptotic core emptiness in a random three-sided assignment market}
\date{17 November 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
In a market with three sectors of $q$ agents each, cross-sector pairs have independent
uniform values on $[0,1]$. A team earns the sum of its three pair values. The minimum subsidy that makes the grand coalition
stable equals the gap $\Dq$ between the fractional and integral assignment optima.
Its expectation is at least $(0.0501-o(1))q^{1/3}$, and $\Dq/\E\Dq$ converges to one
in probability. The probability of a nonempty core lies between $e^{-Cq}$ and
$e^{-c q^{1/3}}$ for some $c,C>0$ and all sufficiently large $q$. Thus the expected
total subsidy grows, while the expected subsidy per agent vanishes.
We also identify the mean-square limit of the fractional deficit divided by $q^{1/3}$.
It is the minimum expected root cost of an invariant perfect matching on the limiting
Poisson hypertree. A recursive matching gives an upper bound of $2.2519$ for this
minimum. A first moment conditioned on the teams incident to each agent gives an
integer deficit of at least $2.302q^{1/3}$ with high probability. Two interval computations
certify the separation.
\end{abstract}
